## Chapter 4.1  
This chapter focuses on presenting various studies regarding preliminary prototypes, customer satisfaction surveys, medication adherence during testing periods, and accessibility.  
##   
## (1) Design Studies / Preliminary Prototypes  
* According to [15], the majority of the systems consist of:  
    * A health record system for hospitals  
    * A patient information system (used by caregivers to monitor the patient)  
    * A smart drug container  
    * A mobile tracking system (an application designed for patients)  
* Some prototypes offer tailored solutions for specific diseases (e.g., audio alarms throughout the house for patients with Alzheimer's, or voice communication for visually impaired individuals) [16] [60].  
* The studies show that a cloud architecture is often the optimal choice for sharing data and information among all stakeholders [37].  
* Modularity: Applicable to pill boxes [44].  
  
**Key Challenges:**  
* Ensuring that the patient has actually ingested the medication  
* Correctly configuring the treatment regimen on the device  
* Maintaining patient autonomy  
* **Domestication [64]:**  
    * Appropriation  
    * Objectification  
    * Incorporation  
    * Conversion  
##   
## (2) Survey Studies  
* Most products were accompanied by a survey study, all of which reported an increase in medication adherence during device usage [68].  
* Several survey studies were conducted to evaluate the accessibility of the devices for patients; overall, there is still room for improvement [63].  
* Devices should also positively impact healthcare staff, caregivers, and family members by alleviating their burden of responsibility [70].
